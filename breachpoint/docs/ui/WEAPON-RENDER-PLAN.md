## 1. Why render rather than draw

**A silhouette rendered from the mesh *is* the weapon. A drawing is a guess at a mesh that
already exists.** The mesh is in the repo, the row already points at it, and the player is
holding it. Drawing an outline of it produces a second, informal, un-versioned description of
the same object — and the moment an artist moves the stock or shortens the barrel, the drawing
is wrong and nothing tells you.

Our own doctrine already said this before this document existed:

> *"An AI painting of a gun that is not the gun in the game is a bug with good lighting."*
> — `ASSET-METHODS.md` §Tier 4

The reduction rules still apply — `ICON-CONSTRUCTION-SPEC.md` §3.5 is not repealed by rendering
(§7). What changes is *where the shape comes from*. The silhouette's outer contour is derived,
not authored. Only the reduction is a judgement call, and a judgement call recorded in a profile
JSON is reviewable in a way a `.png` blob is not.

**The current hand-drawn set is an explicit placeholder** — `HUD-REFERENCE.md` says "Original
art required" against this exact slot, and `ICON-CONSTRUCTION-SPEC.md` §7 forbids copying any
reference weapon silhouette outright. There is nothing to preserve.

**The placeholder-mesh objection, answered.** "The meshes are Epic template guns, so rendering
them just gives us placeholder icons." Correct — and the value of a pipeline is that the second
run is free. When `SM_Rifle` is replaced by a real Breachpoint AR, the icon is regenerated by
re-running one command. A drawing would have to be redrawn, by someone, who would first have to
notice. **Rendering converts an art task into a build step.** That is the entire point, and it
is worth more than the fidelity of the first output.

## 2. The output contract

### 2.1 Size

The slot is **94 × 30.67 at base 1280×720** (`HUD-CAMPAIGN-MEASURED.md` §2) — the largest
element in the tray, and the one that identifies what you are holding.

| Scale | Screen | Slot in px | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1× | 1280×720 | 94 × 30.67 | **layout units, not a texture size.** 30.67 is not an integer |
| 1.5× | 1920×1080 | 141 × 46 | the measured source values — exact, no rounding |
| 2× | 2560×1440 | 188 × 61.33 | |

**We author one master at 2× and let UMG downscale.** Shipping three sizes of the same icon
buys nothing at these dimensions and triples the LFS churn.

**Canvas: 192 × 64. Safe box: 188 × 62, centred, leaving a 2 px transparent margin.**

- 192 and 64 are both divisible by 4, so the texture survives block compression if anyone ever
  changes the compression setting. 188 × 62 is not (62 / 4 = 15.5).
- The margin prevents edge-clamp artifacts when UMG samples at non-integer scales. At 1× the
  2 px margin resolves to ~1 px, which is invisible and harmless.
- The widget draws the brush at the slot's 94 × 30.67 and the margin scales with it. Do **not**
  crop the margin out in the widget.

### 2.2 Format and colour

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Container | PNG, 8-bit RGBA |
| Alpha | **straight (unpremultiplied)** |
| RGB | **`#FFFFFF` on every pixel, opaque or not** |
| Shape | carried entirely by the alpha channel |

**The silhouette must be a single flat colour, not a shaded render.** No ambient occlusion, no
rim light, no gradient, no material colour. Every pixel is white; alpha is coverage. The reason
is mechanical: the widget tints the brush at runtime, and a tint multiplies. Baked shading
multiplied by a tint gives a muddy, non-uniform result that changes hue with the tint, and the
tint is a *token* — it must be changeable without regenerating art.

Set RGB to white **including in fully-transparent pixels**. A PNG with black RGB under zero
alpha bleeds dark fringes when a bilinear sampler averages across the edge.

**Which token does the tinting is unresolved — see §9.2.** The brief names `hud/self`; that
string appears nowhere in this repo. The repo's token table (`ui-presentation` §3) names the
"you" channel `--shield` / `#35D0F2`, while `COMPONENT-SPECS.md` §1 records that the front end
is white/black at varying alpha with colour as accent only. Whichever it resolves to, the
requirement on this pipeline is identical: **ship white, tint at runtime.**

### 2.3 Naming and destination

Follow the repo's existing texture convention (`T_Rifle_N`, `T_Pistol_D`, `T_GridChecker_A`) and
key on the **`DT_Weapons` row name**, not the display name — the row name is what the HUD already
has in hand.

| Artifact | Path |
|---|---|
| PNG (the reviewable projection) | `Tools/render_weapons/out/T_WeaponSil_<Row>.png` |
| Imported texture | `/Game/UI/Icons/Weapons/T_WeaponSil_<Row>` |
| On disk | `Content/UI/Icons/Weapons/T_WeaponSil_<Row>.uasset` |

Concretely: `T_WeaponSil_AR`, `T_WeaponSil_Magnum`, `T_WeaponSil_Rocket`.

**Both the PNG and the `.uasset` are committed.** The `.uasset` is what the game loads; the PNG
is what a human and the critic can actually look at and diff. A `.uasset` alone in the tree with
no PNG beside it defeats the receipt discipline R37 rests on.

`Content/UI/` is **BP10's owner path** (`TICKET_BP10_HUD_FRONTEND.md:21`). A session landing
these must hold that packet or file a `contract_gap` — law 5 is not suspended by having a plan.

## 3. The camera and light rig

**The rig is identical for every weapon. This is the single most important rule in the
document.** A set of icons reads as a family because the camera did not move between them. Per-
weapon camera tweaks produce three icons that are each individually flattering and collectively
incoherent, and the defect is invisible one icon at a time — it only shows in the tray, on a
weapon swap, in motion.

The only per-row knobs are the orientation corrections in §4, and every use of one is a finding
to be justified in the receipt, not a free parameter.

### 3.1 Projection: orthographic, side elevation

- **Orthographic.** Two reasons, both load-bearing:
  1. **Foreshortening scales with object length.** Under perspective a 90 cm rocket launcher and
     a 25 cm magnum receive visibly different distortion at the same camera distance. The set
     stops looking like one set.
  2. **The framing math becomes linear.** Under ortho, bounding-box-units → pixels is a single
     scalar. Fitting is exact arithmetic, not an iterative solve, so the plan can compute and
     print the framing with no editor open — which is what makes §4 reviewable.
- **Side elevation.** Camera on the mesh's lateral axis, looking straight across it, `up = +Z`
  world. A weapon's identity lives in its side profile: barrel length, magazine, stock, optic.
  A three-quarter hero angle carries more information about the model and less about which gun
  it is, and at 94 px wide the extra information is noise.
- Camera forward `(0, -1, 0)`, up `(0, 0, 1)` in UE world axes (X forward, Y right, Z up), with
  the mesh spawned at the origin at identity rotation plus its per-row correction (§4).
- **Roll must be exactly 0.** A tilted weapon reads as a different design language, and any
  tilt is a decision that belongs in the icon spec, not in a camera.

### 3.2 Framing

1. Read the mesh's local bounding box from the asset (`StaticMesh.get_bounds()`).
2. Apply the row's `yaw_deg` / `roll_deg` correction; project the box onto the camera plane.
3. Compute `uu_per_px` **once for the whole set**, from the largest weapon: the largest weapon's
   projected long axis occupies **96 %** of the safe box's 188 px width.
4. Every other weapon uses that same `uu_per_px`.

**This means a magnum is drawn smaller than a rocket launcher, and that is deliberate.**
Relative size is information the player uses, and it is information the mesh already has. The
alternative — fit each weapon individually to the box — makes every icon the same visual weight
and silently discards it. If the magnum turns out to be illegible at true relative scale, the
fix is the per-row `scale_override` in the profile, used knowingly and recorded, not a change
to the rule.

The 4 % margin is against the safe box (188 × 62), which itself sits inside the 192 × 64 canvas.
Total worst-case breathing room: ~5 px at 2×, ~2.5 px at 1×.

### 3.3 The light rig: there isn't one, and that is the point

**No lights. No sky. No post-processing.** A flat silhouette is a coverage mask, not a render of
a lit object. The rig is:

- `ShowFlags.Lighting = False`
- All materials overridden to an unlit constant white. `Content/LevelPrototyping/Materials/M_FlatCol.uasset`
  already exists and is the obvious candidate — **unverified**, its parameters have not been
  read (§9.5).
- Background: black, fully cleared, nothing else in the show-only list.
- Then: white-on-black luminance becomes the alpha channel in post (§7).

This removes an entire class of failure. There is no light direction to get wrong, no exposure
to drift between runs, no auto-exposure adapting to a bright barrel, and no chance of two
weapons rendered on different days receiving different lighting. It also sidesteps UE's
alpha-channel-in-scene-capture behaviour entirely, which is the fiddliest part of this job.

**Anti-aliasing: supersample, never TAA.** TAA is temporal and non-deterministic on a single
frame — it will produce different bytes on identical re-runs, which destroys the receipt's only
diffable artifact. Render at **4× linear** (768 × 256), threshold there, box-downsample to
192 × 64. Disable TAA explicitly (`ShowFlags.TemporalAA = False`, `r.DefaultFeature.AntiAliasing 0`)
rather than trusting a headless default.

**A note for whoever does the item tiles next:** the ~180 item tiles in `ASSET-METHODS.md` Tier 4
*do* need a real light rig — they are shaded hero renders, not masks. This section is not that
rig and must not be reused as one.

## 4. The committed plan file

R37.1: *"A committed plan specifies it first… An MCP call with no committed plan behind it is
hand-placing with a different hand and is a `high` finding."*

### 4.1 Where it lives

`Tools/render_weapons/`, matching `Tools/gen_input/` and `Tools/blockout/`:

| File | What it is | Needs the editor? |
|---|---|---|
| `weapon_render_profile.json` | **the declarative table — the source of truth** | no |
| `render_weapons.py` | executor: loads meshes, frames, renders, post-processes, imports, asserts | **yes** — imports `unreal` |
| `render-weapons.ps1` | wrapper: engine resolution, plugin check, editor guard, logging, ladder exit codes | only when generating |
| `out/*.png` | the rendered projections | no |
| `receipts/*.md` | R37 receipts, one per run | no |

**Deliberately skipped: the separate `*_plan.py` / `selftest_no_editor.py` split** that
`gen_input` and `blockout` both carry. Those exist because their profiles encode dozens of
decisions with cross-cutting contracts (trigger legality, tag families, key collisions). This
profile has three rows and one derived scalar. **Split the plan half out when the table exceeds
~8 rows, or when framing gains any derivation more complex than one multiply.** Recorded here so
the next person knows it was a call and not an oversight.

The wrapper reuses `Tools/_BRLadderCommon.ps1` (`Get-BRLiveEditorProcesses`, `Write-BRBlocked`,
`New-BRLogDir`, the `$BR_EXIT_*` codes). Do not reimplement the guard — `build-input.ps1:180`
and `rename-r26.ps1:88` already carry the correct test, and R36 exists precisely because a
locally-rewritten narrower guard is how this goes wrong.

### 4.2 Schema

```json
{
  "version": 1,
  "source_table": "Content/Data/DT_Weapons.csv",
  "output_dir":  "Tools/render_weapons/out",
  "package_dir": "/Game/UI/Icons/Weapons",

  "canvas": {
    "w": 192, "h": 64,
    "safe_w": 188, "safe_h": 62,
    "supersample": 4
  },

  "rig": {
    "projection":      "orthographic",
    "camera_forward":  [0, -1, 0],
    "camera_up":       [0, 0, 1],
    "fill_fraction":   0.96,
    "unlit_material":  "/Game/LevelPrototyping/Materials/M_FlatCol.M_FlatCol",
    "uu_per_px":       null
  },

  "weapons": [
    {
      "row":            "AR",
      "mesh":           "/Game/Weapons/Rifle/Meshes/SM_Rifle.SM_Rifle",
      "out":            "T_WeaponSil_AR",
      "yaw_deg":        0.0,
      "roll_deg":       0.0,
      "flip_x":         false,
      "scale_override": null,
      "hole_keep":      []
    }
  ]
}
```

| Field | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `source_table` | path | The CSV whose rows this profile mirrors. Asserted, see 4.3 |
| `canvas.supersample` | int | Linear multiplier for the render before downsample. 4 = render 768 × 256 |
| `rig.fill_fraction` | float | Fraction of the safe box the **largest** weapon's long axis occupies |
| `rig.uu_per_px` | float \| null | `null` = derive from the largest weapon and record the derived value in the receipt. A number = pin it, so adding a weapon cannot silently rescale the whole set |
| `weapons[].row` | string | Must equal a `Name` in `DT_Weapons.csv` |
| `weapons[].mesh` | soft path | **Must equal that row's `MeshSoftPath`.** See 4.3 |
| `weapons[].out` | string | Output basename, no extension. `T_WeaponSil_<Row>` |
| `weapons[].yaw_deg` / `roll_deg` | float | Orientation correction, applied to the mesh before framing. Exists because mesh authoring axes are not known (§9.1). **Every non-zero value must be justified in the receipt** |
| `weapons[].flip_x` | bool | Mirror so every weapon points the same way. Which way is a design call nobody has made (§9.6) |
| `weapons[].scale_override` | float \| null | Multiplier on the shared `uu_per_px` for this row only. `null` = the family rule. Non-null is a deliberate departure and must say why |
| `weapons[].hole_keep` | list of strings | Which negative-space holes survive the reduction (§7.3). Empty = solid silhouette. **Max 2** |

### 4.3 The assertion that makes this worth doing

**`weapons[].mesh` must byte-equal the `MeshSoftPath` of the same-named row in
`DT_Weapons.csv`. On mismatch the run refuses, writes nothing, and exits non-zero
(`MESH_PATH_DRIFT`).**

This is the mechanism behind the whole document. If the icon can be rendered from a mesh other
than the one the game equips, the icon can drift from the weapon — which is exactly the failure
mode of a drawing, reintroduced with more steps. Likewise: every row in `DT_Weapons.csv` must
appear in the profile (`ROW_MISSING`), so adding a fourth weapon without an icon fails the run
instead of shipping a blank slot.

Other plan-side refusals, all before any render: `ROW_UNKNOWN`, `MESH_NOT_FOUND`,
`OUT_NAME_SHAPE`, `PACKAGE_OUT_OF_OWNER_PATH`, `SCALE_OVERRIDE_UNJUSTIFIED`,
`HOLE_KEEP_TOO_MANY`.

## 5. Execution — headless, and inside R21 / R29 / R36

### 5.1 The rules this run is subject to

- **R29.1** — one editor instance per project, ever.
- **R29.2** — one driver per editor.
- **R29.3 / R36** — an editor session must not overlap **anything that takes the project lock**,
  which R36 widened from "a build" specifically because *"every editor-driving tool we own is a
  `-run=pythonscript` commandlet that takes the lock without being a build."* **This script is
  exactly that.** It is not a build and it takes the lock.
- **R21** — one build agent at a time; stopping an agent does not stop its children.

**In practice, for the session running this:**

1. No editor window open. The wrapper enforces it via `Get-BRLiveEditorProcesses` and exits
   `BLOCKED` with the process list if one is live. A block is the law firing, not an obstacle.
2. **Do not dispatch a builder while this runs, and do not run this while a builder is live.**
   R29.3 makes the commandlet a party to the build lock. Check for live
   `UnrealBuildTool` / `cl.exe` / `link.exe` *before* launching, not after.
3. If this session is stopped mid-run, the commandlet keeps running and keeps the lock (R21).
   Kill the process tree explicitly or wait for it — do not dispatch over it.

### 5.2 The command line

Safe at any time, launches nothing — validates the profile and prints the whole plan including
the derived `uu_per_px` and every framing number:

```powershell
Tools\render_weapons\render-weapons.ps1 -PlanOnly
```

Renders (needs a free editor, no build in flight, and an LFS lock on any `.uasset` it rewrites):

```powershell
Tools\render_weapons\render-weapons.ps1
Tools\render_weapons\render-weapons.ps1 -DryRun    # loads and frames under the editor, writes nothing
```

Raw headless form, if the wrapper is not being used — **this bypasses the editor guard, so check
by hand first**:

```
<ENGINE_ROOT>\Engine\Binaries\Win64\UnrealEditor-Cmd.exe ^
    <REPO>\Breachpoint.uproject ^
    -run=pythonscript -script="<REPO>\Tools\render_weapons\render_weapons.py" ^
    -stdout -unattended -nosplash -nop4 -NoLiveCoding
```

Switches travel in `BR_RENDERWPN_ARGS` (the wrapper sets it), matching `BR_GENINPUT_ARGS` and
`BR_BLOCKOUT_ARGS` — argument passing into `-run=pythonscript` differs between launch shapes and
the env var is the one that always survives. `ENGINE_ROOT` comes from `Tools/env.local`; there is
no hardcoded engine path anywhere in this folder.

### 5.3 Doctrine: the manifest is truth, the editor is its projection

From the `ue-editor` skill, which is ruling-grade here:

> *"Prefer a **generated, committed Python script** over live interactive editing — by hand or by
> MCP… The manifest/CSV is the source of truth; the editor state is its **projection**.
> Regenerate the projection; never let the projection drift ahead of the source."*

So: **`weapon_render_profile.json` + `DT_Weapons.csv` are the source. The PNGs and the `.uasset`
textures are the projection.** Nobody opens an icon in an image editor and nudges it. A framing
that is wrong is fixed by a number in the profile and a re-run. If you find yourself in
Photoshop, the fix belongs in the JSON.

Corollary: the script must be **idempotent**. A re-run with an unchanged profile and unchanged
meshes must produce byte-identical PNGs and report zero texture saves. Determinism is achievable
here in a way it is not for `.uasset` bytes (Unreal reserialises package metadata on every save)
— which is precisely why the PNG is the artifact the receipt hashes.

### 5.4 Remote Control is not the landing mechanism

The Remote Control API (WebControl, HTTP :30010) is legitimate for poking a running editor
during iteration — "does this yaw look right?" — and is explicitly **not** how these land.
Landing is the committed script, headless, every time.

## 6. The receipt

R37.2: *"A receipt names every call and its result, and is committed with the asset. The critic
cannot diff a `.uasset`; the receipt is what it reviews instead."*

R37 also names the enforcement gap this rests on: `guard_laws.py` gates `Edit`/`Write` by
`file_path`, and an editor-side asset write has neither. **The receipt is the only control there
is.** A session that lands these textures without one has defeated it.

### 6.1 Format

Two files per run, both committed with the assets:

| File | Format | For |
|---|---|---|
| `Tools/render_weapons/receipts/render-<UTC-stamp>.md` | Markdown | humans and the critic |
| `Saved/WeaponRender/render-<UTC-stamp>-<verdict>.json` | JSON | machine diffing, matching `gen_input`'s `Saved/InputGen/` convention |

**Written as the run proceeds, appended per call.** A receipt reconstructed from memory
afterwards is not a receipt (`TICKET_BP19` states this outright).

### 6.2 What it must record

**Run header**
- UTC start / end, host machine, editor build version, commandlet PID
- `git rev-parse HEAD` at launch, and whether the tree was dirty
- sha256 of `weapon_render_profile.json`
- sha256 of `DT_Weapons.csv` (the assertion in §4.3 is only meaningful against a pinned table)
- the full resolved plan, including the derived `uu_per_px` and which weapon it came from
- the exact command line, including every switch

**Per weapon**
- row name, mesh soft path, and the `MeshSoftPath` it was compared against
- mesh bounding box as read from the asset, in uu
- resolved orthographic width, `yaw_deg` / `roll_deg` applied, `scale_override` if any **and its
  stated justification**
- output PNG path, byte size, **sha256**
- opaque-region bounding box in px, and its fill fraction against the safe box
- ink fraction (opaque area / canvas area) — cross-check against `ICON-CONSTRUCTION-SPEC.md`
  §3.5's measured 0.32–0.57 band for small weapon silhouettes
- minimum feature width in px at 1× display size, and the min-feature verdict (§7.2)
- hole count before reduction, each hole's area, and which were kept
- destination package path, every texture setting applied, and the resulting `.uasset` LFS oid

**Every call, verbatim**
- every `unreal` API call that loaded, mutated, rendered, wrote or saved — name, arguments,
  return value
- **including refusals and errors.** A receipt showing only the calls that worked is a
  advertisement, not a record
- the post-write assertion pass: what was read back, and whether it matched

**Verdict** — per weapon and overall, one of `PASS` / `FAIL` / `BLOCKED`, with reasons. A partial
run is `FAIL`, not `PASS` with notes.

## 7. Post-processing to hit the construction spec

**A raw render is not a HUD icon.** `ICON-CONSTRUCTION-SPEC.md` is not suspended by the source
being a mesh — it is exactly what the mesh does not know.

### 7.1 Threshold to a flat silhouette — automatable

At the **supersampled** resolution (768 × 256), threshold luminance > 0.5 to a binary mask, then
box-downsample 4×4 to 192 × 64 to produce alpha in [0, 255]. Force RGB to `#FFFFFF` everywhere.

Order matters. Thresholding *after* the downsample gives a jagged edge; downsampling *after* the
threshold gives a hard-edged silhouette with clean coverage anti-aliasing, which is what the
spec's "solid silhouette" means. Five lines with PIL or numpy.

### 7.2 Minimum-feature check — automatable as a check, not as a fix

`ICON-CONSTRUCTION-SPEC.md` §6: hard corners, butt terminals, uniform weight, and nothing that
disappears at shipped size.

Test at **1× display size** (94 × 30.67 — evaluate at 94 × 31), not at the 2× master:

1. Downsample the alpha to 94 × 31.
2. Binarise at 0.5.
3. Erode with a 3×3 structuring element.
4. Any connected component that vanishes was thinner than ~2 px. Any negative-space hole that
   closes was narrower than ~2 px.
5. Report every one, with its area and location. Fail the run (`MIN_FEATURE`) if any exist.

Expect this to fire on: sight posts, antennas, sling loops, thin rails, the gap between a trigger
guard and a grip. **The check is automatic; the fix is not.** The options are per-row `flip_x` /
`yaw_deg` (sometimes the feature is an edge-on artifact), deleting the feature, or accepting it
— and choosing is a human call recorded in the profile.

### 7.3 The outline → silhouette reduction — **needs a human eye**

`ICON-CONSTRUCTION-SPEC.md` §3.5, measured from the reference:

> *"Below ~70 px on the long axis, line-art weapons become filled silhouettes with **at most two
> negative-space holes** — and those two holes are chosen for recognition (trigger guard,
> magazine well), not for accuracy."*

Our long axis is 94 px at 1× — near enough to that threshold that the rule applies. Interior
detail counts in the reference collapse 6→0, 17→2, 32→1 across the size drop.

**A render hands you every hole the mesh has**: scope mounts, stock cutouts, rail slots, ejection
ports, the gap under a carry handle. Most of them are noise at 31 px tall.

| Step | Automatable? |
|---|---|
| Find and measure every hole in the alpha (connected components of the background inside the silhouette) | yes |
| Fail if more than 2 survive (`HOLE_COUNT`), listing each with its area and centroid | yes |
| **Decide which ≤2 holes identify this weapon** | **no — human** |
| Apply the decision on every subsequent run | yes, from `hole_keep` |

This is the shape the doctrine wants: the *judgement* is made once by a person, written into
`weapon_render_profile.json` as text, and applied mechanically forever after. The decision lives
in a reviewable diff; it does not live in a `.png`.

Naming holes stably is the fiddly part. Suggested: `<edge>_<ordinal-by-area>`, e.g.
`interior_1`, `interior_2` sorted by descending area, with the receipt printing the full sorted
list every run so a hole that changes rank after a mesh update is visible rather than silently
swapped. **Unverified** — this survives a mesh edit only as well as the ordering does, and a
better key may be needed.

### 7.4 What is not automatable, listed honestly

- Which two holes mean "this is the AR".
- Whether a feature that fails the 2 px test should be thickened, deleted, or the weapon
  re-yawed to show it better.
- Whether the true relative-scale rule (§3.2) leaves the magnum too small to read, and whether
  the fix is `scale_override` or accepting it.
- Whether the resulting silhouette is *recognisably that weapon* — §8.3.

## 8. Verification

### 8.1 Side-by-side against the drawn placeholder

**Blocked as written: there is no committed drawn placeholder** (§0). What can be done today:

- Compare against the **measured slot**: the opaque region must sit inside 94 × 30.67 at 1× and
  fill ≥ 90 % of the safe box's constraining axis for the largest weapon.
- Compare ink fraction against `ICON-CONSTRUCTION-SPEC.md` §3.5's measured band for small weapon
  silhouettes (0.32 / 0.61 / 0.49 across three reference weapons). A render landing at 0.08 is
  wireframe-thin; at 0.85 it is a blob. Both are failures the number catches.
- If the Figma `HUD / Elements` page carries a drawn silhouette node, export it at 2× and diff
  visually. **Not verified to exist** — nobody has checked, and it is not in git.

### 8.2 The 2 px minimum-feature test

Exactly §7.2, run by the script, result in the receipt, per weapon. This is the objective gate
and it either passes or it does not.

### 8.3 Legibility at final size on a busy background

The test that actually matters, and the one no script can score:

1. Composite the icon at **94 × 30.67**, tinted with the resolved token, at the tray's measured
   position — **x 1124.00, y 639.33 at base 1280×720** — over an arena screenshot, not over grey.
   `take_high_res_screenshot` from `BR_Arena01` gives a real background.
2. Repeat at 1920×1080 (the icon at 141 × 46).
3. Look at it. Then look at the three together, as they appear on a weapon swap.

**The identity test, which is the real bar:** show the three icons at final size to someone who
knows the three weapons, without labels. If they cannot say which is which, the reduction is
wrong — regardless of how faithful the render is to the mesh. Fidelity to the mesh is the
*method*; recognition is the *requirement*, and §3.5's reference data shows the reference set
threw away literally every pixel to keep it.

### 8.4 Reproducibility

Re-run with an unchanged profile. **The PNG sha256s in the receipt must match.** This is the
check the critic can actually perform, and it is the one thing this pipeline gives that no
hand-authored icon ever will.

Judge the `.uasset` by "saved: none" on a second run, **never** by a binary diff — Unreal
reserialises package metadata and object GUIDs on every save, so identical bytes are not a
thing to expect. This is the same honesty `gen_input`'s README records about determinism.

## 9. Open questions and blockers

Stated plainly. None of these were resolvable from the repo, and none should be guessed at
during execution — each needs a decision or an experiment.

1. **The mesh authoring axes are unknown.** Which local axis is the barrel on `SM_Rifle`,
   `SM_Pistol`, `SM_GrenadeLauncher`? Where are their pivots — muzzle, grip, origin? Nothing in
   the repo records it and the `.uasset` files cannot be read without the editor. The `yaw_deg` /
   `roll_deg` / `flip_x` fields exist for exactly this, and **their values must be determined by
   a first render pass and written back into the profile with the receipt showing why.** Until
   then every value in the shipped profile is a guess. This is the single largest unknown here.

2. **The tint token is unresolved, and the name in the brief does not exist in this repo.**
   `hud/self` returns zero hits. `ui-presentation` §3 names the "you" channel `--shield`
   (`#35D0F2`); `COMPONENT-SPECS.md` §1 records the front end as white/black at varying alpha
   with colour as accent only. Three possibilities: the token was renamed and the docs did not
   follow, it exists only in Figma, or the tray silhouette is meant to be plain white. **This
   does not block rendering** — ship white either way — but it blocks the widget and it blocks
   §8.3's composite.

3. **There is no consumer, and building one crosses three owner paths.** To put these on screen
   you need: a `TSoftObjectPtr<UTexture2D>` field on the weapon row in
   `Source/Breachpoint/Data/BRDataRows.h`; an `IconSoftPath` column in `Content/Data/DT_Weapons.csv`
   plus a reimport; and an icon field on `UBRVM_Combat` in `Source/Breachpoint/UI/BRViewModels.h`.
   The first two are outside any UI packet's owner path — **that is a `contract_gap` for whoever
   picks this up, not something this plan may fix** (law 5). Law 3 also settles the shape: the
   reference is **soft**, it lives in the data table, and there is no `ConstructorHelpers`
   anywhere in it.

4. **All three meshes are Epic template placeholders.** The icons this produces are honest
   pictures of what is in the game today and every one of them will be regenerated. Worth saying
   out loud so nobody reviews the first output as final art — review the *pipeline*, and review
   the framing rule, because those are what survive.

5. **The render mechanism itself is unverified.** No editor was opened. Specifically unproven:
   that `M_FlatCol` can serve as the unlit white override (its parameters have not been read);
   that a `SceneCaptureComponent2D` with `ShowOnlyActors` + `ShowFlags.Lighting = False` produces
   a clean white-on-black frame in 5.8 headless; that `export_render_target` writes the PNG we
   expect; that TAA is fully off under `-run=pythonscript` with the flags named. **Expect the
   first run to be a spike that corrects §3.3, and expect §3.3 to be edited afterwards** — that
   is the intended shape, not a failure.

6. **Which way does a weapon point?** Muzzle left or muzzle right is a design call nobody has
   made, and the reference cannot be copied for it (`ICON-CONSTRUCTION-SPEC.md` §7 forbids taking
   any weapon silhouette from it, and direction is part of a silhouette). `flip_x` is a per-row
   field, but the *rule* must be one direction for the whole family and it needs an owner.

7. **Does the tray show the stowed weapon too?** `HUD-CAMPAIGN-MEASURED.md` measures exactly one
   silhouette slot, and `UBRVM_Combat` carries both `ActiveWeaponName` and `StowedWeaponName`.
   If the stowed weapon gets an icon it likely needs a second, smaller size — which would change
   §2.1 from one master to two, or force a min-feature re-check at the smaller size. Unmeasured.

8. **The `hole_keep` naming key is fragile.** Ordering holes by descending area is stable until a
   mesh edit reorders them, at which point a saved decision silently applies to the wrong hole.
   The receipt printing the full sorted list every run makes the change *visible*; it does not
   make it *safe*. A better key may be needed and none is proposed here.

## 11. SPIKE CHECKLIST — what a human must observe on the first run

The pipeline is now written: `Tools/render_weapons/` (see its README). **None of the editor
half has ever been run.** This section is the ordered list of observations the first run
exists to produce. Work it in order — each item makes the next one meaningful, and stopping
at the first failure is correct, because a wrong answer to item 1 invalidates every number
after it.

**Do not treat a failing first run as a defect in the tooling.** §9.5 said this run would
correct §3.3, and §9.1 said the orientation values in the table are guesses. The deliverable
of the first run is **answers**, not icons.

**Before you start:** no editor window open, no builder live (R21/R29.3/R36 — the commandlet
takes the project lock without being a build). Run
`Tools\render_weapons\render-weapons.ps1 -SelfTest` and `-PlanOnly` first; they need no
editor and they are the only part of this that is proven. Then:
`Tools\render_weapons\render-weapons.ps1 -DryRun` before the real run — it loads and frames
under the editor and writes nothing, so it answers items 1, 2 and 6 at zero risk.

Keep the run's receipt (`Tools/render_weapons/receipts/render-<stamp>.md`) and the raw
`Tools/render_weapons/out/*_ss4x.png` open while you work through this.

### 11.1 Does the mechanism exist at all? (answered by `-DryRun`)

- [ ] **Did the commandlet get an editor world?** The receipt either shows an
      `EditorActorSubsystem` or refuses naming assumption **A1**. If there is no world, the
      capture strategy in §3.3 is wrong at the root — the fix is a different mechanism
      (offscreen world, thumbnail renderer, `MoviePipeline`), not a parameter, and §3.3 must
      be rewritten before anything else on this list is worth doing.
- [ ] **Did all three meshes load?** `MESH_NOT_FOUND` here means the soft path in
      `DT_Weapons.csv` is wrong, which is a data finding, not a rendering one.
- [ ] **Did every `unreal` API name resolve?** Each refusal names the binding it wanted and
      why. Fix the name in `render_weapons.py`, note the correction in the receipt.

### 11.2 The authoring axes — **the single largest unknown (§9.1)**

This is why the spike exists. Read the answers off the raw 768×256 frame, not off the
downsampled icon.

- [ ] **Which local axis is the barrel?** Look at each weapon at `yaw_deg=0`.
      - A full side profile → the barrel is on local **X**; the identity guess was right.
      - A short stub, roughly as wide as the receiver → the barrel points at or away from the
        camera (local **Y**); the row needs `yaw_deg = ±90`.
      - A vertical weapon → the barrel is on local **Z**; the row needs `roll_deg = ±90`.
      Record the *observation*, not just the correction: "SM_Rifle rendered 40 px wide at
      yaw 0, so the barrel is on Y" is what makes the number in the table reviewable later.
- [ ] **Which sign?** Once the long axis is on screen, is the muzzle left or right? Note it
      per mesh. The family must all point the same way (§9.6) and **that direction still has
      no owner** — pick nothing here; record what each mesh does and file the decision.
- [ ] **Where is the pivot?** The weapon is spawned at the origin and the camera is centred on
      it, so a mesh whose pivot is at the muzzle or the grip renders **off-centre at the
      correct size**. If a weapon is the right size but hugs an edge, that is a pivot finding,
      not a framing one — and §3.2's framing math has no centring term, so it is a real gap
      that needs a decision (recentre on the bounds origin, or accept per-mesh offset fields).
- [ ] **Is the same axis the barrel on all three meshes?** They are three separate Epic
      template assets and there is no reason for them to agree. If they disagree, that alone
      justifies the per-row `yaw_deg` field existing.

### 11.3 Does the flat-white override actually read?

- [ ] **Is the render pure white on black, with no shading anywhere?** Sample a few pixels in
      the middle of the weapon body in the raw frame. Anything that is not `255,255,255` —
      a gradient, an ambient-occlusion darkening, a coloured tint — means the override did not
      take, and §2.2's contract (RGB `#FFFFFF` on every pixel, shape carried entirely by
      alpha) is not being met.
- [ ] **Did `M_FlatCol` load, and is it actually unlit?** §3.3 named it as the *obvious
      candidate* and marked it unverified — nobody has read its parameters. A
      `MATERIAL_NOT_PARAMETERISED` finding in the receipt means whatever colour the asset
      carries is what rendered.
- [ ] **Did the override reach every material slot?** A weapon with an unlit white body and a
      shaded scope is one slot that was missed.
- [ ] **Is the background fully black and fully empty?** Any sky, fog, grid or second actor in
      frame means `ShowOnlyActors` / `PRM_USE_SHOW_ONLY_LIST` did not apply, and the
      luminance→alpha step in §7.1 will turn that background into silhouette.

### 11.4 Is TAA off?

- [ ] **Does the receipt show every ShowFlag being set, or `SHOWFLAG_UNSETTABLE` warnings?**
      The flag names in `render_weapons.py` are assumption **A4** and several may not be
      exposed to Python at all.
- [ ] **The decisive test: run it twice and compare the PNG sha256s** in the two receipts.
      **Identical bytes on an unchanged plan is the only proof that AA is deterministic**
      (§8.4). TAA is temporal and non-deterministic on a single frame, so if the two hashes
      differ, TAA (or some other temporal effect) is still on regardless of what the flags
      claimed, and the receipt's one diffable artifact is worthless until it is fixed.
- [ ] **Look at the edges of the raw frame.** A smeared or ghosted contour is TAA; hard
      aliased stair-steps at 768×256 are *correct* here — the supersample-then-threshold order
      in §7.1 is what turns them into clean coverage.

### 11.5 Does the 2 px minimum feature survive at 94 × 30.67?

The output contract's real bar, and the one the spike does **not** automate — §7.2's erode
test is `NOT IMPLEMENTED IN THE SPIKE`. Do it by eye at true size.

- [ ] **View the final PNG at 1× display size (94 × 31), not zoomed.** Not at the 2× master,
      and not in an image viewer that is scaling it up.
- [ ] **What disappears?** Expect: sight posts, antennas, sling loops, thin rails, the gap
      between the trigger guard and the grip. List every one with roughly where it was — this
      list is the input to the §7.3 reduction decision, which is a human call.
- [ ] **How many interior holes are there, and do they still read?** §7.3 allows at most two,
      chosen for *recognition* (trigger guard, magazine well), not accuracy. A render hands
      you every hole the mesh has.
- [ ] **Is the magnum still legible at true relative scale?** §3.2 says a magnum drawn smaller
      than a launcher is information, not a bug, and that `scale_override` is a knowing
      departure — decide, do not drift.
- [ ] **The identity test (§8.3):** show the three at final size, unlabelled, to someone who
      knows the three weapons. If they cannot say which is which, the reduction is wrong no
      matter how faithful the render is to the mesh. **Fidelity is the method; recognition is
      the requirement.**
